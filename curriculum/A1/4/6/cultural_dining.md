# Lesson 6: Cultural Context - Dining Customs in Spanish-Speaking Countries

**Cultural Notes:**

## Meal Times

Spanish-speaking countries often have different meal schedules than English-speaking countries:

**Spain:**
- **Desayuno** (Breakfast): 7-9 AM - Usually light (coffee, toast)
- **Almuerzo** (Lunch): 2-3 PM - Main meal of the day
- **Cena** (Dinner): 9-11 PM - Lighter than lunch

**Mexico:**
- **Desayuno** (Breakfast): 6-8 AM
- **Comida** (Main meal): 1-3 PM - The biggest meal
- **Cena** (Dinner): 8-10 PM

**Argentina:**
- **Desayuno** (Breakfast): 7-9 AM
- **Almuerzo** (Lunch): 12-2 PM
- **Merienda** (Afternoon snack): 4-6 PM
- **Cena** (Dinner): 9-11 PM or even later!

## Restaurant Etiquette

**Ordering:**
- Waiters may not bring the check until you ask: "La cuenta, por favor"
- Tipping is usually 10% in most countries
- It's common to spend 2+ hours at dinner

**Common Phrases:**
- "¿Qué me recomienda?" - What do you recommend?
- "Está delicioso" - It's delicious
- "Un poco más, por favor" - A little more, please

## Regional Food Customs

**Spain:**
- **Tapas** culture - small dishes shared with drinks
- **Siesta** time - many restaurants close 4-8 PM

**Mexico:**
- **Tortillas** with every meal
- Spicy food is normal - ask "¿Está picante?" (Is it spicy?)

**Argentina:**
- **Asado** (barbecue) is a social event lasting hours
- **Mate** tea is shared among friends

## Real-Life Scenarios

**Scenario 1: At a Restaurant**
- Waiter: "¿Qué va a tomar?"
- You: "Una agua mineral, por favor"
- Waiter: "¿Y para comer?"
- You: "¿Qué me recomienda?"

**Scenario 2: At a Friend's House**
- Host: "¿Quieres más comida?"
- You: "Está muy rica, pero ya estoy lleno. Gracias"
- Host: "¿Un cafecito?"
- You: "Sí, por favor"

**Cultural Tip:** Food is central to social life. Meals are times for conversation and connection, not just eating!
