
# Lesson 3: Conditional Perfect

**New Words:**
*   habría, habrías, habría, habríamos, habríais, habrían - conditional of "haber"
*   ido - gone
*   dicho - said
*   si - if

**Grammar:**
The conditional perfect tense is used to talk about an action that **would have happened** in the past, but did not, usually because a certain condition was not met. It expresses hypothetical or unreal situations in the past.

It is a compound tense, formed with the **conditional** tense of the auxiliary verb **haber** + a **past participle**.

(Remember, `haber` uses the irregular stem `habr-` for the conditional, same as the future).

**Key Rule:** The two parts of the verb, `haber` and the past participle, are **never separated**.

This tense is most often found in "if" clauses (or implied "if" clauses).

Examples:
*   Si hubiera sabido, **habría ido** contigo.
    *   (If I had known, I **would have gone** with you.)
    *   *The reality is I didn't know, so I didn't go.*

*   Yo no **habría dicho** eso.
    *   (I **would not have said** that.)
    *   *Implies: ...if I were in your situation.*

*   Con más tiempo, **habríamos terminado** el proyecto.
    *   (With more time, we **would have finished** the project.)
    *   *The reality is we didn't have more time, so we didn't finish.*

It can also be used, like the future perfect, to express **probability or conjecture in the past**.
*   Eran las diez y Marta no estaba. Se **habría ido** a casa.
    *   (It was ten and Marta wasn't there. She **had probably gone** home.)
