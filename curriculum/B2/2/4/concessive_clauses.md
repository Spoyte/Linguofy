
# Lesson 4: Concessive Clauses (Aunque, a pesar de que)

**Grammar:**
Concessive clauses present an obstacle or difficulty to the action in the main clause. They are introduced by conjunctions like `aunque` (although, even though, even if) and `a pesar de que` (despite the fact that).

The verb in the concessive clause can be in the **indicative** or the **subjunctive**, and the choice depends on the certainty of the information.

**1. `Aunque` + Indicative**
Use the indicative when the clause states a **fact** or something the speaker believes to be true. It translates to "although" or "even though."
*   **Aunque llueve**, voy a salir. (Even though it is raining, I am going to go out.)
    *   *Fact: It is currently raining.*
*   **Aunque estoy cansado**, terminaré el trabajo. (Although I am tired, I will finish the work.)
    *   *Fact: I am tired.*

**2. `Aunque` + Subjunctive**
Use the subjunctive when the clause is **hypothetical** or **not yet a fact**. It translates to "even if."
*   **Aunque llueva** mañana, saldré. (Even if it rains tomorrow, I will go out.)
    *   *Hypothetical: It may or may not rain tomorrow.*
*   Te ayudaré **aunque no me lo pidas**. (I will help you even if you don't ask me to.)
    *   *Hypothetical: You may or may not ask.*

**3. `A pesar de que`**
This phrase is almost always followed by the **indicative** because it presents a known fact or obstacle. It means "despite the fact that."
*   **A pesar de que estudió** mucho, no aprobó el examen. (Despite the fact that he studied a lot, he didn't pass the exam.)
*   Logramos llegar a tiempo **a pesar de que había** mucho tráfico. (We managed to arrive on time despite the fact that there was a lot of traffic.)

**4. `A pesar de` + Infinitive**
If there is no change in subject, you can simplify `a pesar de que` by using `a pesar de` + infinitive.
*   **A pesar de estudiar** mucho, no aprobó. (Despite studying a lot, he didn't pass.)
