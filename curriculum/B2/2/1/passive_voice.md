
# Lesson 1: The Passive Voice

**Grammar:**
The passive voice is used when you want to emphasize the recipient of an action rather than the person or thing performing the action (the agent). The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

You have already learned one way to do this: the **passive "se"** (`Se venden casas`). The other, more formal way is the **true passive voice**.

**Formation:**
**subject + ser (in any tense) + past participle + por + agent**

The key rules are:
1.  The verb **ser** is conjugated in the tense of the original active sentence.
2.  The **past participle** must agree in gender and number with the new subject.
3.  The original subject (the agent) is introduced by the preposition **por**.

**Active to Passive Example:**
*   **Active:** Miguel de Cervantes **escribió** *Don Quijote*. (Cervantes wrote Don Quijote.)
    *   *Subject: Cervantes, Verb: escribió (Preterite), Object: Don Quijote*
*   **Passive:** *Don Quijote* **fue escrito** por Miguel de Cervantes. (Don Quijote was written by Cervantes.)
    *   *New Subject: Don Quijote, Verb: ser in preterite (fue) + participle (escrito), Agent: por Cervantes*

**More Examples:**
*   **Active:** El presidente **firmará** la ley mañana. (The president will sign the law tomorrow.)
*   **Passive:** La ley **será firmada** por el presidente mañana. (The law will be signed by the president tomorrow.)
    *   *Note: `firmada` is feminine to agree with `la ley`.*

*   **Active:** Los romanos **construyeron** muchos acueductos. (The Romans built many aqueducts.)
*   **Passive:** Muchos acueductos **fueron construidos** por los romanos. (Many aqueducts were built by the Romans.)
    *   *Note: `construidos` is masculine/plural to agree with `muchos acueductos`.*

**Usage:**
The true passive voice is much more common in written and formal Spanish (news, literature, technical writing) than in everyday conversation, where the passive "se" is generally preferred.
