
# Lesson 6: Augmentatives and Diminutives

**Grammar:**
Spanish uses suffixes to alter the meaning of a noun, adjective, or adverb. The two most common alterations are diminutives (to express smallness or affection) and augmentatives (to express largeness or impressiveness).

**1. Diminutives**
Diminutives add a sense of smallness or endearment. They are incredibly common in conversation. The most common suffix is **-ito/-ita**.
*   **-ito/-ita:** Add to words ending in -o, -a, or a consonant.
    *   perro -> **perrito** (doggie)
    *   casa -> **casita** (little house)
    *   árbol -> **arbolito** (little tree)
*   **-cito/-cita:** Add to words ending in -e or more than one syllable ending in -n or -r.
    *   café -> **cafecito** (little coffee)
    *   madre -> **madrecita** (mommy)
    *   joven -> **jovencito** (youngster)
*   **-ecito/-ecita:** Often used for one-syllable words ending in a consonant.
    *   flor -> **florecita** (little flower)
    *   pan -> **panecillo** (bread roll) - *irregular*

**Examples:**
*   ¿Quieres un **cafecito**? (Would you like a (little) coffee?)
*   Mi **abuelita** es muy simpática. (My dear grandma is very nice.)
*   Espera un **momentito**. (Wait a little moment.)

**2. Augmentatives**
Augmentatives add a sense of largeness, impressiveness, or even contempt. The most common suffixes are **-ón/-ona**, **-azo/-aza**, and **-ote/-ota**.
*   **-ón/-ona:** The most common augmentative.
    *   soltero (bachelor) -> **solterón** (confirmed bachelor, old bachelor)
    *   silla (chair) -> **sillón** (armchair)
*   **-azo/-aza:** Can imply a blow or strike with the object. Can also be used to mean "great" or "cool".
    *   cabeza (head) -> **cabezazo** (head-butt)
    *   coche (car) -> **cochazo** (awesome car)
    *   gol (goal) -> **golazo** (amazing goal)
*   **-ote/-ota:** A very common augmentative.
    *   libro (book) -> **librote** (big, heavy book)
    *   grande (big) -> **grandote** (huge)

**Examples:**
*   ¡Qué **cochazo** tienes! (What an awesome car you have!)
*   Ese hombre **grandote** es mi hermano. (That huge man is my brother.)
*   Ten cuidado, ese perro es un **perrazo**. (Be careful, that dog is a huge dog.)
