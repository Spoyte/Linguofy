
# Lesson 5: Expressing "If Only" with Ojalá

**Grammar:**
`Ojalá` is an Arabic loanword that expresses a strong wish or hope. You've seen it used with the present subjunctive to mean "I hope..." But it can also be used with past subjunctive tenses to express a sense of longing for something different, translating to "if only..." or "I wish..."

**1. `Ojalá` + Present Subjunctive**
This expresses a **hope for the future**. It's a desire for something to happen.
*   **Ojalá que llueva** café en el campo. (I hope it rains coffee in the countryside.)
*   **Ojalá (que) gane** mi equipo. (I hope my team wins.)
*   **Ojalá (que) tengamos** suerte. (I hope we're lucky.)

**2. `Ojalá` + Imperfect Subjunctive**
This expresses a **wish for the present to be different**. It's a hypothetical statement about a current, contrary-to-fact situation. It means "If only..."
*   **Ojalá hiciera** más calor ahora. (If only it were warmer now.)
    *   *Reality: It is not warm now.*
*   **Ojalá fuera** más alto. (I wish I were taller.)
    *   *Reality: I am not taller.*
*   **Ojalá tuvieras** la respuesta. (If only you had the answer.)
    *   *Reality: You don't have the answer.*

**3. `Ojalá` + Past Perfect (Pluperfect) Subjunctive**
This expresses a **regret about the past**. It's a wish that something in the past had been different. It means "If only... had happened."
*   **Ojalá hubiera estudiado** más para el examen. (If only I had studied more for the exam.)
    *   *Reality: I did not study more.*
*   **Ojalá no hubiera llovido** ayer. (I wish it hadn't rained yesterday.)
    *   *Reality: It did rain yesterday.*
*   **Ojalá hubiéramos comprado** las entradas antes. (If only we had bought the tickets earlier.)
    *   *Reality: We did not buy them earlier.*

In summary, the tense used after `ojalá` reflects the timeframe of the wish:
*   Future hope -> Present Subjunctive
*   Present wish -> Imperfect Subjunctive
*   Past regret -> Past Perfect Subjunctive
