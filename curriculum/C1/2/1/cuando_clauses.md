
# Lesson 1: Temporal Clauses with "Cuando"

**Grammar:**
Temporal clauses are clauses that indicate when an action takes place. The conjunction `cuando` (when) is the most common way to introduce them. The verb tense in the clause after `cuando` depends on the time frame of the action.

**1. Habitual Actions (Past or Present)**
If the action is habitual or a general statement, use the **indicative**.
*   **Present Habitual:** Cuando **hace** frío, siempre **llevo** un abrigo. (When it's cold, I always wear a coat.)
*   **Past Habitual:** Cuando **era** niño, siempre **jugaba** fuera. (When I was a child, I always used to play outside.)

**2. Completed Past Actions**
If the action in the `cuando` clause is a single, completed event in the past that interrupts another, both verbs are typically in the **indicative** (imperfect for the background action, preterite for the interrupting one).
*   **Leía** un libro cuando **sonó** el teléfono. (I was reading a book when the phone rang.)

**3. Future Actions**
This is the most important distinction for advanced learners. When `cuando` refers to an action that has **not yet happened**, you must use the **present subjunctive** in the `cuando` clause. The main clause will use the future or a command.

*   **Incorrect:** *Cuando llegaré a casa, te llamaré.*
*   **Correct:** Cuando **llegue** a casa, te **llamaré**. (When I get home, I will call you.)
    *   *Getting home is a future event, not yet a reality, so it requires the subjunctive.*

*   Cuando **termines** tu tarea, **puedes** ver la televisión. (When you finish your homework, you can watch TV.)
*   Avísame cuando **sepas** algo. (Let me know when you know something.)

**Summary:**
*   `Cuando` + Past Event -> **Indicative**
*   `Cuando` + Habitual Event -> **Indicative**
*   `Cuando` + Future/Pending Event -> **Subjunctive**
